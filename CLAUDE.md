# CLAUDE.md

Guidance for Claude Code in this repository.

## Project

MModule is the repository of MoDule (called MD Drums up to 0.1.1; the code keeps that name: `mdDrums`, `mdDrumsPlugin`): the Elektron Machinedrum's synthesis engines as a plug-in built like The Usual Suspects' Gearmulator plug-ins (**TUS**: Osirus, OsTIrus, Vavra, Xenia, Nodal Red 2x, JE-8086) and edited like **Overbridge**. One window edits the machine tab by tab: each track's machine, synthesis, effects, routing and LFO, the master effects, the mix, the Kit. Every parameter is a host parameter that Live can automate, and Live's clips sequence the 16 tracks. OS 1.63's code runs piecewise: its voice and master programs on emulated DSP56300s (`source/dsp56300/`), its machine, LFO and smoothing routines in an emulated 68k (`source/mc68k/`); the OS itself, its boot and its sequencer do not run.

The TUS architecture, with OsTIrus as the reference (`osTIrusJucePlugin/` over `virusJucePlugin/` in upstream Gearmulator, not in this repository): a thin plug-in over the emulated machine, its parameters and their MIDI messages declared in the JSON (`midipackets`, `regions`), presets in the Patch Manager, a skin whose controls bind to parameters. MoDule keeps its parameters in its JSON but maps them to the Machinedrum's messages in C++ (`mdProtocol/mdautomation.*`, `mdDrums/mdDrumsMessages.*`), and keeps its Kits in its own Bank rather than the Patch Manager.

**Latency** is a requirement, measured rather than assumed: the time from a host note to the sound at the plug-in's output, and its jitter. A change to the trig path or the render pipeline reports both, before and after.

Gearmulator MD (md-mm) drives the intact firmware over MIDI: notes trigger tracks, host parameters reach it as CC, Kits travel as SysEx. Engines without the UC were ruled out in `.scratch/parallel-transport/issues/07-go-no-go.md` on CPU and effort: the sequencer costs about 6 points of UC, dropping the UC leaves DSP1 (the busiest core) as heavy, and the DSPs' HI08 protocol would need reverse engineering machine by machine. **Decided otherwise on 2026-10-06: the plug-in moves to the engines without the OS.** The protocol is now documented in public (`.scratch/uc-less-engines/`), and Machinemodule's engine (sd88me, AGPL-3.0), imported as `source/elektron/md/mdEngine/`, runs OS 1.63's engines without it: DSP2 emulated, the OS's own machine, LFO and smoothing routines called in a 68k emulator, DSP1's track chain and mix in C++. Measured against the full firmware: the same samples (`mdEngineFirmwareTest`), about a tenth of the CPU, 0.48 ms of note-to-sound latency against ~5.4 ms (Machinemodule's measure, from the 32-sample block that holds the note; from the note itself, 1.16 ms on average and 0.68 ms of jitter on TRX-BD, whose voice sounds a block after its trigger), and no 14 s silent boot. Since ticket 19 MoDule's Hits are sample-accurate: every Track's share of the mix waits the rest of the engine's block, so a Hit sounds 31 samples after its note plus its voice's own start, and since ticket 36 every voice waits for the latest, TRX-BD's, whose voice sounds a block after its trigger: each machine's start (`Engine::voiceStart`, measured by `mdDrumsEngineTest` on every machine's own output: 4 to 8 samples for most, 12 for ROM, 36 for TRX-BD and TRX-S2) is held back to `Engine::AlignedStart`, so that every Hit sounds `Engine::HitDelay` samples after its note on Main and on its Out alike (83, 1.88 ms, no jitter at 44.1 kHz; the Outs wait Dynamix's 16 samples as Main does). X.20's ANTI-CLICK, a global setting in the menu (off by default, 64 or 128 samples, ticket 33 of the User Machines map), holds the Hit of a machine X.20 fades while its Track's raw voice ramps out as X.20's mixer ramps it, and delays every other Hit as much, the latency reported growing by the fade (`Engine::hitDelay`). TRX-XT, TRX-XC and TRX-CB start later still (40, 68, 102) and are not held back, as on the Machinedrum. A Choke cuts its target exactly where the cutting Hit sounds, a lower Track at the engine's next tick instead, as `HostModel` reports each block's cuts (ticket 38). Since ticket 24 the master effects run the mixer DSP's own master section on a second emulated DSP56300 (`mdEngine/engine/MasterEngine.*`) with the words the OS's own code computes, sample for sample the firmware's (`mdEngineFirmwareTest`); Main comes 16 samples after the Outs, Dynamix's look-ahead, as on the machine. The combined work is AGPL v3 (`NOTICE.md`). The full firmware still runs here, as the oracle: `mdLib` and the tests in `mdLibTest/`.

The repository split on 2026-10-06 from [c0remusic/gearmulator-md-mm](https://github.com/c0remusic/gearmulator-md-mm), which keeps the full-machine plug-ins (sequencer editor, BIBLIO's patterns, pattern chains), and became MModule on 2026-10-17: [c0remusic/gearmulator-md-drums](https://github.com/c0remusic/gearmulator-md-drums)' history filtered with `git filter-repo` to MoDule, the firmware oracle and what they build on. The other Gearmulator synths, md-mm's full-machine plug-in (`mdJucePlugin`) and its tests are not in this repository; gearmulator-md-drums keeps release 0.1.0. The Monomachine is out of this project: the Monomachine paths left in `mdProtocol` and `mdLib` serve `mdLib` alone.

## Where things are

- `source/elektron/md/mdDrums/`: without JUCE. `mdDrums::Engine`, mdEngine as the plug-in drives it (flash lookup, 32-sample block rendering, Links, Chokes, LFOs); `mdDrums::Device`, the `synthLib::Device` over it; `mdDrumsMessages.*`, MoDule's own messages, its state format and the host parameters as the Machinedrum's messages. `mdDrumsPlugin/`: the processor, the Controller, the editor (its custom elements and views), the parameter JSON, the skins, and `Design/` (the approved v22 mockup and its boxes).
- `source/elektron/syntakt/stDrums/` (without JUCE) and `stDrumsPlugin/`: ST Drums (manufacturer `C0rm`, code `Stdr`), `syntakt::Syntakt` with 16 tracks as a plain JUCE AudioProcessor with JUCE's generic editor. `stDrumsFirmware.*` reads the user's OS 1.41 `.syx` (SysEx, ELE3, the LZ codec) into sections 3 and 7, from `Documents/c0remusic/ST Drums/roms`; `stDrumsPlayer.*` plays notes 36-51 as trigs at the source sample they fall on and gives Main (A's and B's direct and FX buses summed) and Track 01-15 (the stems), resampled from 48 kHz by synthLib's MameHq where the host runs at another rate (its group delay reported as the latency); `stDrumsParameters.h` holds the frozen string IDs (`t01_machine`, `t01_note`, `t01_level`, `t01_p01`-`t01_p53` by internal id, `fx_p17`-`fx_p34`, `tempo`).
- `source/elektron/md/mdEngine/`: the engines without the OS (`EngineT` in `engine/MdEngine.h`), with its origin and local changes in `README.md`. Musashi's entry points serve one CPU class per executable, so nothing links it with `mdLib`'s `Microcontroller`: tests that compare the two run in separate processes.
- `source/elektron/syntakt/stFast/`: the Syntakt's machines rewritten for speed (`syntakt::fast`: 32-bit float, the 16 Tracks as SSE2 lanes, blocks of 32 at 48 kHz, the machine's own rate), BD MODERN, SD BASIC, CY ALLOY and PC CARBON so far, their shared stages in `stFastStages.*` and their quality bench in `stFastBench.h`. The Syntakt always renders at 48 kHz (spec §11.2, amended 2026-10-27: it depends on its rate throughout, fold-over, one-sample feedback, period-2 locks); `fast::Host<Machine>` (`stFastResample.*`) brings it to a 44.1 kHz host through a polyphase resampler (Q11 in `stFastResampleTest`, flat within 0.1 dB to 18 kHz, 24 host samples of latency; a trig lands on the machine's 48 kHz block grid, up to 28.4 host samples late, which ticket 11 settles) and refuses other rates until ticket 16. It reads nothing of the firmware: its tables are made by formula (`stFastTables.*`), exact or fitted within a stated error that `stFastTablesTest` enforces against section 7; stFast links no stEngine, only its tests do. Each machine's test judges it against the exact port (`stEngine`) at 48 kHz, its 44.1 kHz output against its 48 kHz one, and its voice with the feedback table at 0, by the quality tests of `.scratch/elektron-drums/research/19-qualite-audio.md` §11 as ticket 05 set them.
- `source/elektron/md/mdVoice/`: the voice DSP's machines ported to C++ without an emulator (`md::voice::Voice`: the 64-word state block and the dispatcher of `.scratch/uc-less-engines/machinedrum-kit/08-dsp2-voice-abi.md`), GND-- and GND-IM so far. `mdVoicePortTest` gives it and mdEngine's `VoiceEngine` the same slots from the OS's coefficient functions and compares every voice's samples, then checks that it finds a sample changed on purpose.
- `source/elektron/md/mdProtocol/`: the machines' MIDI without an emulator, linked by `mdLib` and MoDule. `mdautomation.*` maps host parameters to CC; `mdmachines.*` is the machine table; `mdsysexautomation.*` holds the SysEx codecs (Kits, patterns, `$5B`, master effects, `$62`); `mdkit.*` is the whole Kit (`MdKit`), its dump writer and reader and the Link and Choke messages (`$65`, `$66`); `mdkitchange.*` decodes, encodes and applies, without allocating, the messages that change one thing of the Kit (`$5B`, `$5D-$60`, `$62`, `$65`, `$66`) as OS 1.63 reads them, which the Device and `messages::parameterMessage` use and the allocating encoders wrap (ticket 39).
- `source/elektron/md/mdLib/`: the whole machine, MoDule's oracle (from md-mm). `mdhardware.*` runs the UC and the DSPs and delivers MIDI; `mddevice.*` is the `synthLib::Device`. Nothing of MoDule links it.
- Tests: next to their code (`mdDrums/*Test.cpp`, `mdDrumsPlugin/*Test.cpp`, `mdProtocol/mdProtocolKitTest.cpp`), and the firmware comparisons in `mdLibTest/` (`mdEngineFirmwareTest` and its fixtures, `mdKitDumpFirmwareTest`, `mdAutomationMidiTest`).
- Shared Gearmulator layers: `synthLib/` (Device, MIDI), `jucePluginLib/` (parameters, MIDI Learn, Patch Manager), `jucePluginEditorLib/`, `juceRmlUi/` (RmlUi inside JUCE).

## Build

Windows, Visual Studio 2022, build directory `temp/cmake_vs22`, VST3 only:

```powershell
cmake . -B temp/cmake_vs22 -G "Visual Studio 17 2022" -DBUILD_TESTING=ON `
  -Dgearmulator_BUILD_JUCEPLUGIN_AU=OFF -Dgearmulator_BUILD_JUCEPLUGIN_CLAP=OFF -Dgearmulator_BUILD_JUCEPLUGIN_LV2=OFF `
  -Dgearmulator_BUILD_JUCEPLUGIN_Standalone=OFF -Dgearmulator_BUILD_JUCEPLUGIN_VST2=OFF -Dgearmulator_BUILD_FX_PLUGIN=OFF
cmake --build temp/cmake_vs22 --config Release -j 6 --target mdDrumsPlugin_VST3
```

A release build adds `"-DMD_DRUMS_VERSION=X.Y.Z"`, quoted: unquoted, PowerShell cuts the argument at its first dot and the build says version 0, which `mdDrumsUpdateTest` refuses. The macOS build (universal VST3 and AU) is `.github/workflows/md-drums-macos.yml`, run by hand.

- `jucePluginLib/pluginVersion.cpp` recompiles on every build (version timestamp, MSB8065 by design): a build with no change still compiles one file.
- Run firmware executables by hand only under a wall-clock guard (`Start-Process -PassThru` + `WaitForExit(ms)`, kill on timeout).
- Worktrees (`.claude/worktrees/*`) start without submodules or build directory. Check the submodules out from this checkout's module repositories, offline, then configure a fresh `temp/cmake_vs22`:

```bash
M=C:/dev/MModule/.git/modules/source
git -c protocol.file.allow=always \
  -c submodule.source/dsp56300.url=$M/dsp56300 -c submodule.source/JUCE.url=$M/JUCE \
  -c submodule.source/mc68k.url=$M/mc68k \
  -c submodule.source/3rdparty/RmlUi.url=$M/3rdparty/RmlUi -c submodule.source/3rdparty/freetype.url=$M/3rdparty/freetype \
  -c submodule.source/asmjit.url=$M/dsp56300/modules/source/asmjit -c submodule.dlg.url=$M/3rdparty/freetype/modules/dlg \
  submodule update --init --recursive
```

`git submodule status` then shows `-` on every submodule although each is checked out: the URLs were given on the command line, not written to `.git/config`.

## Gate

Before every commit and push, one script (its test list is the gate's: add a new test there):

```powershell
pwsh -NoProfile -File scripts\windows\gate.ps1 -Log <file>
```

It builds the gate's targets, runs ctest on them with `--output-on-failure`, and ends on `GATE GREEN` (exit 0) or
`GATE RED` (exit 1). The log holds the build's errors, the machine's load before the tests, each test's line, the failing
tests' own messages (the whole ctest output beside it, `<log>.ctest.txt`), and the elapsed time. It keeps the display on
while it runs (`mdDrumsVst3HostTest` captures the editor's window, which a sleeping display leaves blank).

The timing tests (`mdDrumsVst3HostTest`, `mdDrumsEngineTest`) bound the audio thread's wall-clock time, so other programs
that take the processor make them fail: on 2026-10-21, with Ableton Live playing (2.6 cores) and another session's
survey on six processes, `mdDrumsVst3HostTest` failed 5 runs of 10 on "a block stalled the audio thread" (worst blocks
12 to 63 ms against the 10 ms bound). The gate writes the load it measured; a timing test that fails alone runs once
more, and passing then, the gate is green and says "timing test failed under load, passed on its second run". Any other
failure, or a timing test failing twice, is red. Gate on a quiet machine whenever possible.

Green is 41 passed out of 41: `stEngineOracleTestScalar` and `stMainOracleTestScalar` run the Syntakt's two oracle tests again with the AVX2 kernels off (`SYNTAKT_NO_AVX2=1`), and ctest adds the 4 fixture tests `mdEngineFirmwareTest` requires (`mdEngineFirmwareHits`, `mdEngineFirmwareRomBank`, `mdEngineFirmwareGroups` and `mdEngineFirmwareMaster` boot the firmware to record the hits, the voice DSP's UW bank, the Link and Choke scenarios and the master effect scenarios with the mixer DSP's memory, which `mdEngineFirmwareTest` compares the engine with).

`MD_X20_OS` names the user's own MDX X.20 OS file, kept beside the ROMs on this PC and never in the repository: `mdX20OsTest` reads it where it is set, and runs only its LZMA and refusal checks without it; `mdX20BuiltinsTest` plays X.20's built-in Machines from it, and nothing without it; `mdX20PortTest` compares the port of their routines with X.20's own run in a 68k (exit 77 without it).

The ROM is required. A firmware test exits 77 without its ROM and ctest reports it Skipped, which proves nothing: the gate takes a skipped test as failed and names it ("skipped, which the gate takes as failed"). On 2026-10-25 it had said `GATE GREEN` with the 9 Syntakt tests skipped. It sets each `SYNTAKT_*` variable left unset from its `-SyntaktData` folder (`C:\dev\elektron-work` by default: `syntakt-1.41\` holds the sections of the user's OS 1.41, `vectors\` and `vectors-main\` the vectors and expected outputs, `references\` the references and the sample set; `SYNTAKT_OS_SYX` from `-SyntaktOs`, by default the `Syntakt_OS1.41.syx` of Elektron's download in `Downloads\Syntakt OS 1.40A\Syntakt OS\`) where the files are, and writes those it could not set at the top of the log; nothing of it enters the repository.
- The firmware tests read `GEARMULATOR_MD_FIRMWARE_BIN`; MoDule's tests find the flash image as `mdDrums::Engine::findFlashImage` says.
- `stEngineOracleTest` reads `SYNTAKT_SECTION7` (the user's OS 1.41 section 7) and `SYNTAKT_VECTORS` (the Syntakt port's vector files, `;`-separated), both outside the repository, and `SYNTAKT_SAMPLES` (the synthetic sample set SP TWINSHOT's vectors were recorded with, `twinshot_samples.bin`).
- `stVoiceCpuTest` reads `SYNTAKT_SECTION7` and `SYNTAKT_REFERENCES`, a directory outside the repository holding the original's `boot_sram.bin` and `bd_modern_note60.wav` (its header says what they are).
- `stMainOracleTest` reads `SYNTAKT_SECTION3` (the user's OS 1.41 section 3, the main CPU's) and `SYNTAKT_MAIN_VECTORS` (the vector files of CPU #1's ported routines, ST1V and ST1S, `;`-separated), both outside the repository; with `SYNTAKT_MAIN_OUTPUTS` (the original outputs of the block and mix sequences, `block_sequence.sto;mix_sequence.sto` in that order), `SYNTAKT_SECTION7` and `SYNTAKT_SAMPLES` it also checks the eight tracks end to end.
- `stSyntaktTest` reads `SYNTAKT_SECTION3`, `SYNTAKT_SECTION7`, `SYNTAKT_SAMPLES` and `SYNTAKT_SCENARIO` (the original firmware's outputs for its scenario, `syntakt_scenario.sto`, outside the repository): `syntakt::Syntakt` plays the scenario through its interface and its 14 outputs must match sample for sample. The expected file is remade by `stSyntaktTest --log calls.txt`, then `tools/syntakt3.py calls.txt syntakt_scenario.sto --samples twinshot_samples.bin` (about 5 minutes); `--wav out.wav` also writes the scenario's mix to listen to.
- `stDrumsFirmwareTest` and `stDrumsVst3HostTest` read `SYNTAKT_OS_SYX` (the user's `Syntakt_OS1.41.syx`, outside the repository): the first unpacks its sections 3 and 7 (checked by their FNV-1a hashes, and section 3 against `SYNTAKT_SECTION3` and section 7's words against `SYNTAKT_SECTION7` where set); the second copies it into the roms folder of a data folder of its own (`GEARMULATOR_DATA_ROOT`) and plays the built ST Drums VST3 against `syntakt::Syntakt` driven directly. ## Tests

- A test that creates a processor makes it for tests (`mdDrums::Processor(true)`): no update check, no MCP server, whose firewall prompt Windows would show for every new executable.
- A harness that paces a fake audio callback spin-waits to each deadline: a process without a visible window runs on the 15.6 ms timer, and a sleeping audio thread invents dropouts.
- A new test's `main` sets `setvbuf(stdout, nullptr, _IONBF, 0)`: `_IOLBF` crashes the Microsoft runtime at startup (0xc0000409).
- An editor test is the editor's clock (`Editor::setClock`): it steps `Editor::frame` as a display tick, and an RmlUi update runs the frame first, as in the plug-in.
- MoDule's two host tests share `mdDrumsPlugin/mdDrumsHostDriver.h` (header-only, JUCE alone: the VST3 test does not link the plug-in): notes played in blocks, a parameter automated, a state restored, and the scenarios of ticket 34 that `mdDrumsProcessorTest` (in process) and `mdDrumsVst3HostTest` (through VST3 hosting) both run, each adapter counting what the plug-in tells the host as its channel reports it.

## Critical implementation details

- **State:** `Device::getState()` appends to `_state` with `insert()`, never `assign()`: the Plugin layer has already written its version header there.
- **Host:** what the plug-in changes by itself reaches the host one of two ways (ticket 34, measured in Live 12.2.1). An edit: JUCE turns a parameter's listeners called on the message thread into `performEdit`, which Live records while it records and adds to the device's panel. A re-read: `PresetChange`, then `Controller::tellHost` (`updateHostDisplay` with `programChanged`, VST3's `restartComponent(kParamValuesChanged)`; this JUCE's `getParamNormalized` reads the parameter), after which Live, while it records, writes automation for every parameter on its panel, changed or not (Live sets no `kRecording` to wait for). Values changed in bulk (a Kit, a state, a machine's 24 parameters) are re-read; one parameter following another (a relay and its parameter of the Track shown) is not told, since as an edit Live takes the relay for the parameter last touched in the editor; the relays are edits without a gesture when the Track shown changes; nothing at load. What the user changes outside the parameters (a Sample, a User Machine, a Link, a Choke, the Kit's name) asks the host to mark the set modified (`Processor::markStateChanged`, VST3 `setDirty`), which Live 12.2.1 may ignore (ticket 11 of the Samples map, its judgment in Live pending).
- **Device lock:** `Plugin::withDeviceLocked` pauses rendering, which the host shows as a CPU spike when it runs on every frame or click: the editor reads the Telemetry and the parameters instead.
- **Threads:** RmlUi DOM changes happen on the JUCE message thread. From audio or MIDI callbacks, post with `juce::MessageManager::callAsync`, the lambda guarded by the static instance-set pattern against use-after-free.
- **Editor frame:** MoDule's views change the document only in an RmlUi event (a click, a key, a drag: they write at once) or in `Editor::frame`, both under the document's access lock, which the OpenGL thread renders under. In the frame each view's `step` reads what it shows (parameters, Telemetry, Controller, Bank), compares it with what it drew, writes only what differs and says whether it wrote; no view keeps a timer or a parameter listener. The frame runs before every RmlUi update (`RmlComponent::evPreUpdate`), so what an event changed shows in the frame that draws the event; at the display's rate while the window shows (one `juce::TimedCallback`, for the host's automation, the Telemetry, a Track shown by Push or a state); at once when a click shows another Track (`Editor::setCurrentPart`) or a file has been chosen. A frame with nothing new writes nothing, and costs about a microsecond (`mdDrumsSkinTest`'s "frame").
- **Animation:** the software renderer ignores RmlUi transforms (`RendererJuce::SetTransform` stores the matrix, nothing reads it). Draw what moves with `juceRmlUi::ElemCanvas` (see `mdDrumsMeterView.cpp`, `mdDrumsHitView.cpp`), and hide elements with `display`, never a zero scale.
- **Environment overrides** in `mdLib` (`MD_*`, `MDMM_*`) go through `mdLib/mdenv.h` (`envOverride`, `envNumber`, `envCount<T>`), where unset, empty and unparsable all count as unset: PowerShell's `SetEnvironmentVariable($n, $null)` leaves an empty variable that child processes see.

## Code conventions

- Tabs (size 4), 120 columns, braces on their own line, namespace content indented; `.clang-format` in `source/`.
- PascalCase classes; camelCase functions, variables and namespaces; `m_` members, `_` parameters (`void func(int _param)`).
- Early returns over nesting. C++17.
- Commit subjects are imperative sentences that say what the change does for the machine ("Write a step edit into the pattern edited, not into A01"); the body says why, with the numbers measured.
